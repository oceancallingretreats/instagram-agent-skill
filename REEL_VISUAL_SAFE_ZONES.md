# Instagram Reel visual safe zones — Ocean Calling

Source: user-provided transcript of a tip attributed to Sofi Pasos Marketing, 31 October 2026. No exact pixel coordinates or percentage boundaries were supplied; do not invent them.

## Mandatory pre-publish framing and typography checks

1. Keep the main animal, person, face, or point of attention in a clearly visible central area. Place important visual focus near the middle, especially before punch-ins, zooms or reframing. Recheck the full animation, not only the first frame.
2. Reserve space for the written hook and on-screen subtitles/captions. Keep text away from Instagram's top and bottom interface overlays, the right-side control stack, and any other danger zones. Do not assume that the in-app post description is the same as burned-in subtitles.
3. Prefer a comfortably central text-safe area and validate against the actual Instagram Reel preview. Dangerous or avoid-at-all-costs areas depend on viewport, crop, UI and placement; there are no verified measurements in the source transcript.
4. If footage has a busy background, add contrasting text outline/stroke (or subtle shadow/contrast treatment) so words remain readable. Check contrast on bright and dark frames.
5. On a punch-in or zoom, preserve the key subject in the center and keep hook/subtitles within the visible safe region throughout.
6. Final QA: inspect the cover, opening hook, captions, subject framing, zoom transitions and Instagram UI overlaps on a phone before publishing. This is a design checklist, not evidence of a guaranteed reach or retention improvement.

Use alongside existing Hook Intelligence, Reel Intelligence, wildlife accuracy/welfare/rights checks and audio rule: no added background music when speech/voiceover is present, to prioritize Instagram translation.
